## Pál's theorem

[Pál’s theorem: a circumscribed regular hexagon](https://beniamin-bogosel.github.io/constant-width-problems/#cw-pal-hexagon) stays in **Generalities**, where it can support several later chapters. It was already present; this pass names it explicitly, adds the side length and area, and cites Martini–Montejano–Oliveros Theorem 15.2.3 alongside the author's blog proof.

For width w, all six sides of the regular hexagon support the body, opposite sides are w apart, the side length is w/√3, and the area is √3 w²/2. A straight-sided hexagon is not itself a constant-width body: its maximum width is 2w/√3. This avoids ambiguity in “unit width hexagon.”

## Source versions and checks

- **Hynd, version 1 (2023):** the local 27-page PDF of [arXiv:2303.04359v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04359v1) contains both the support-function variational route in §3 and the approximation/shortest-arc route in §4. These are the two approaches requested by the user.
- **Hynd, version 2 (2024):** [arXiv:2303.04359v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04359v2) retains the polygon route in §3 and adds the explicit shortest-chord comparison in Lemma 3.3. The variational section is absent. Version-specific IDs and locators prevent these from being conflated.
- **Bogosel:** the local `BL2D_optim.tex`, *New variational arguments regarding the Blaschke–Lebesgue theorem*, was read for finite-class existence (§2), shape derivatives (§3), and center variations (§4). The linked [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13808) supplies a portable reference; local labels and fingerprints identify the manuscript actually used. The exercises derive the two-vertex specialization with explicit CCW cyclic indexing.
- **Yaglom–Boltyanskii:** the available §7 scan was checked for Exercises 7-10–7-12 (printed p. 74), the approximation discussion (pp. 76–80), and 7-19–7-20 (p. 80). The shortest-arc solution was studied through Hynd's exposition, which attributes it to 7-20; this pass does not claim to have inspected the original book's solution pages.

The variational route was rewritten to keep curvature moment constraints explicit. In the first Hynd version, the proposed perturbation before Lemma 3.4 subtracts a first harmonic to enforce closure, but the following box-bound calculation drops that harmonic. Such a projection can change curvature on saturated regions and cannot simply be declared admissible. The statements and signs around that lemma also need care. Our exercises instead use a two-moment linear minimization argument and allow the corresponding translating vector to be chosen by duality. The three-patch exercise gives a separate moment-preserving proof of zero-one curvature. These are independently written proof drafts, not a verbatim transcription or a claim about the reason for the later revision.

Two elementary factors were checked directly: the ODE endpoint slopes for endpoint values 1/2 have magnitude **(1/2) tan(ℓ/2)**, and the curvature of `(h+δ)/(1+2δ)` is **(ρ+δ)/(1+2δ)**. The exercises use these normalizations.

The full shortest-arc surgery lemma is longer and more geometric than the final area bookkeeping. It is supplied explicitly in that exercise, with a direct pointer to Hynd, rather than hidden behind a claim that admissibility is obvious. The equiangular exercises similarly distinguish what is proved from the additional lower comparison needed for the original 7-12 proof.
